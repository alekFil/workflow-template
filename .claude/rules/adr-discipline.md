# adr-discipline

An ADR is one of the most important decisions of the project, not a log of every
change. Two levels:

- **ADR** – a file in `<DECISIONS_DIR>/` (`.claude/workflow.conf`). Passes the
  4-point checklist below.
- **Commit message** – everything else: tactical fixes, renames, cleanup. The reason
  goes into a `Why:` section of the commit.

## Who creates an ADR

Only the owner, with an explicit `/record`. Other skills (`/architect`, `/dev`,
`/go`, `/sync`, `/summary`, `/retro`) never write an ADR. When they meet a decision
that may deserve one, they list it as an **ADR candidate**: in the `## ADR
candidates` section of the task card or in their report. The owner decides.

With `DECISIONS_DIR=none` (contribution to someone else's project) ADRs are not kept
at all: the upstream project has its own process.

## Checklist (all four must be "yes")

1. Alternatives considered with substantive analysis. For "do / don't do" decisions –
   the reason for this option over the opposite.
2. Explicit trade-off between alternatives.
3. The decision shapes product or process behavior over 6 months or more.
4. A reader six months from now, without context, benefits from knowing *why*, not
   *what*.

## File

`<DECISIONS_DIR>/YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>.md`, the date of the decision and a short
lowercase `a-z0-9-` slug. The ID is unique from birth, so parallel branches do not
collide and references never change.

```markdown
---
title: "Use SQLite by default"
status: accepted
date: 2026-11-26
---

# Use SQLite by default

## Context

## Decision

## Alternatives

## Consequences
```

- `status`: `accepted`, `superseded` or `rejected`. A superseded ADR stays in place
  with `status: superseded` and `superseded-by: <id of the new ADR>`. Nothing is
  moved to an archive.
- References: by ID, for example `2026-11-26-sqlite-by-default`.
- `<DECISIONS_DIR>/README.md` is generated by `.claude/scripts/workflow.sh
  adr-index`. Never edit it by hand; on a merge conflict, rerun the command.

ADRs written before 2026-11-26 carry `legacy-id: ADR-NNN` in frontmatter; the index
shows it, so old references like "ADR-030" in commits and notes stay resolvable.
